Building or rebuilding along the **Santa Monica and Malibu coastline** means working within a regulatory and environmental context that inland Los Angeles construction simply does not encounter. Coastal Commission review and salt-air material durability are not minor details here — they are central to how these projects are planned and priced from the start.

## Coastal Zone Review: An Additional Layer, Not a Substitute

Properties within the California Coastal Zone are subject to review beyond standard city or county building permits.

### The Coastal Development Permit process

Depending on the property's location and the local jurisdiction's Local Coastal Program status, a Coastal Development Permit may be required in addition to standard building permits — reviewed either by the local jurisdiction or by the California Coastal Commission directly. In Malibu, much of the coastal-zone review is handled at the city level under its certified Local Coastal Program, while certain categories of development — particularly those closer to the immediate shoreline or involving specific environmentally sensitive habitat areas — can still require direct Coastal Commission review or appeal jurisdiction.

### This review often shapes the timeline more than construction itself

For many coastal projects, the coastal permitting process is the single longest variable in the overall schedule, which is why it needs to be factored into project planning from the very beginning rather than treated as a formality. Public notice requirements, potential public hearing scheduling, and the possibility of an appeal period after local approval all add time that a standard inland building permit simply doesn't carry.

### Beach access, viewshed, and bluff-top considerations

Coastal Commission review considers factors that have nothing to do with structural code — public beach access easements, protection of coastal viewsheds from public roads, and, for bluff-top or beachfront lots, geologic setback from an eroding bluff or the mean high tide line. Any of these can affect where a home can sit on the lot or how tall it can be, independent of what local zoning would otherwise allow.

## Why Salt Air Changes Material Specification

Proximity to the ocean introduces a durability consideration that inland Los Angeles construction does not need to address to the same degree.

### Standard fasteners and metals corrode faster

Salt-laden air accelerates corrosion in standard steel fasteners, some aluminum products, and certain finishes. Coastal-grade, corrosion-resistant materials are specified specifically to perform over the long term in this environment. Standard hot-dip galvanized fasteners that would perform reliably for decades on an inland property can show meaningful corrosion within just a few years within the first several hundred feet of the shoreline, which is why stainless steel or specially coated fastening systems are the standard specification for framing connectors, deck hardware, and railing systems on true beachfront construction.

### Exterior finishes need to be selected with exposure in mind

Paint systems, wood treatments, and metal finishes all perform differently near the coast than they do further inland, which is why coastal material selection is its own specialized consideration rather than a simple upgrade from standard specifications. Window and door hardware, exterior light fixtures, and even HVAC condenser units benefit from coastal-rated corrosion protection — an inland-spec condenser unit installed on a beachfront property can show meaningful coil corrosion well before its expected service life is up.

### HVAC and mechanical systems need protection too

Salt air is hard on mechanical equipment generally, not just visible finishes. Coastal properties benefit from condenser coil coatings, more frequent maintenance intervals, and in some cases equipment placement decisions that reduce direct salt spray exposure — considerations worth discussing with your mechanical contractor at the design stage rather than after a unit has failed prematurely.

## Structural Considerations for Wind and Wave Exposure

Beachfront and near-shore properties in Malibu also face structural design requirements tied to wind exposure and, for oceanfront lots, wave and flood hazard considerations under FEMA flood zone designations. Homes in a designated coastal high hazard area may require elevated foundations, breakaway wall systems at lower levels, and specific engineering for wind uplift on the roof structure — requirements that go well beyond standard residential wind load provisions used further inland.

## What This Means for Project Planning

Coastal construction is entirely achievable — it just requires planning for realities that inland projects do not face.

### Build coastal review into the schedule from day one

Owners planning a Santa Monica or Malibu project should expect coastal review to be a defined phase of the project timeline, with realistic time allocated rather than assumed away. We recommend beginning the coastal permitting conversation in parallel with architectural design, not after design is complete, since certain design decisions — height, bulk, and setback from the bluff or shoreline — are exactly what coastal review scrutinizes most closely.

### Specify materials for the environment, not just the design

Material selection for coastal properties should account for long-term performance in a salt-air environment as a core requirement, not an afterthought layered on at the finish stage. A material or fastening system that looks identical to its inland counterpart in a showroom can perform very differently after a decade of direct ocean exposure, which is why we treat corrosion resistance as a specification requirement, not an upgrade option, for true coastal properties.

## Working With a Contractor Experienced in Coastal Permitting

Coastal Development Permit applications require a specific type of documentation and a familiarity with how Coastal Commission staff and local coastal-zone planners evaluate a given site — geologic setback calculations, viewshed analysis, and biological assessments for sensitive habitat areas are not things a typical inland building permit application ever touches. A design and construction team with direct experience navigating this process for Santa Monica and Malibu properties can anticipate the specific documentation a given site is likely to require, rather than discovering gaps in the application after submission triggers a request for additional information — a common cause of extended review timelines on coastal projects handled by teams without direct local experience.

## Cost Realities for Coastal Construction

Beyond the extended permitting timeline itself, coastal construction carries real cost premiums tied to material specification, site logistics, and, in many cases, more complex foundation engineering for bluff-top or beachfront conditions. Corrosion-resistant fastening systems and coastal-rated windows and mechanical equipment cost more than their standard counterparts, and site access on many Malibu and Pacific Coast Highway-adjacent properties is more constrained than a typical inland lot, affecting delivery logistics and staging. Owners budgeting for a coastal project should build in both the extended timeline and the material premium from the earliest planning stages, rather than applying an inland cost-per-square-foot assumption to a fundamentally different building environment.

## Rebuilding After Storm or Erosion Damage

Coastal bluff and beachfront properties face erosion and storm-related risks that inland properties simply don't encounter, and rebuilding after such damage often requires updated geologic and coastal engineering analysis reflecting current bluff or shoreline conditions — not simply a permit to reconstruct what was there before. This is worth understanding proactively, since a property's shoreline or bluff-top position can change over time in ways that affect what future reconstruction is permitted to look like.
