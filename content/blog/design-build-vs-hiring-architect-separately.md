One of the earliest decisions in any significant Los Angeles construction project is not about design at all — it is about **project delivery**. Should the architect and builder be one coordinated team, or should the owner hire an independent architect and contractor separately? Both models produce excellent homes. They allocate risk, communication, and accountability in genuinely different ways.

As a firm that offers [custom home construction](/services/custom-home-construction-los-angeles) under both delivery models, we think owners deserve a clear-eyed comparison rather than a pitch for whichever model a given firm happens to offer.

## How Design-Build Actually Works

In a design-build project, the architectural design and construction responsibility sit under one team, working together from the start.

### Cost feedback happens earlier

Because the builder is involved from early design, cost implications of design decisions surface in real time — rather than after a full drawing set is complete and sent out for competitive pricing. This tends to produce more reliable budget alignment earlier in the process.

### Accountability is consolidated, for better and worse

With one team responsible for both design and construction, there is no gap for design and construction responsibility to fall between two separate parties. The tradeoff is that the owner needs to evaluate that single team's design capability directly, since there is no independent design check built into the structure.

## How the Traditional Architect-Then-Contractor Model Works

In this model, the owner hires an architect independently, completes design, and then selects a contractor — either through negotiation or competitive bid — to build it.

### The architect works exclusively for the owner

An independently retained architect has no financial relationship with the builder, which some owners value as an independent voice through design and into construction administration.

### Coordination becomes the owner's responsibility

Without a single unified team, the owner (often with the architect's help) needs to actively manage the handoff between design and construction, including resolving any gaps or conflicts that surface once a contractor begins pricing and building the design.

## How to Actually Decide

The right model depends less on which is theoretically better and more on the specific team and project in front of you.

### Evaluate the design-build team's actual design portfolio

If considering design-build, look closely at the design quality of that specific team's past work — not just their construction credentials. Design-build only delivers on its promise when the design side is genuinely strong.

### Consider how involved you want to be as owner

The separate-architect model generally requires more active owner coordination between two parties. Design-build requires more owner diligence up front in selecting the right team, since so much accountability sits in one place.
