**Seismic retrofitting** is a familiar term for older, modest Los Angeles homes on raised foundations — but the conversation looks different for luxury properties, where structural systems are often more complex and the stakes of getting retrofit work wrong are higher.

Our structural coordination work through [custom home construction](/services/custom-home-construction-los-angeles) and [luxury modernization](/services/luxury-modernization) regularly includes seismic assessment, because protecting a significant investment means understanding what is actually holding the structure up.

## What Determines Retrofit Scope

Every retrofit conversation starts with the same question: what is the home's existing structural system, and how does it perform under seismic load?

### Foundation type matters most

Homes on raised, cripple-wall foundations — common in older Los Angeles construction — are typically the most straightforward retrofit candidates, since foundation bolting and cripple wall bracing address well-understood failure modes. Homes on slab foundations or with more complex structural systems require a different, more individualized engineering evaluation.

### Era of construction shapes the starting point

Homes built before modern seismic code requirements were adopted often lack basic connections between the structure and its foundation that are now standard. Understanding when and how a home was originally built is a key input into scoping retrofit work.

## What Retrofit Work Actually Involves

Retrofit scope ranges from targeted foundation work to more extensive structural intervention, depending on what the engineering assessment finds.

### Foundation bolting and cripple wall bracing

For homes with the common raised-foundation vulnerability, retrofit work typically includes bolting the structure to its foundation and bracing cripple walls — the short stud walls between the foundation and the first floor — to prevent lateral collapse.

### More complex homes may need broader structural intervention

Luxury homes with large open spans, extensive glazing, or architecturally complex structural systems may require more involved engineering solutions beyond standard foundation retrofit measures.

## Why Combining Retrofit With a Remodel Often Makes Sense

Retrofit work and remodel work frequently overlap in the areas of the home they need to access.

### Shared access reduces disruption and cost

If a remodel already involves opening walls, floors, or crawl space areas, incorporating retrofit work into the same phase avoids opening those same areas twice — once for the remodel and again for a separate retrofit project.

### Engineering coordination benefits both scopes

A structural engineer evaluating a home for remodel-related changes is well positioned to also assess seismic performance at the same time, producing a single coordinated plan instead of two disconnected ones.
