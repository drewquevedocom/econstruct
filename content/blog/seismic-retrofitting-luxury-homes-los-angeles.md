**Seismic retrofitting** is a familiar term for older, modest Los Angeles homes on raised foundations — but the conversation looks different for luxury properties, where structural systems are often more complex and the stakes of getting retrofit work wrong are higher.

Our structural coordination work through [custom home construction](/services/custom-home-construction-los-angeles) and [luxury modernization](/services/luxury-modernization) regularly includes seismic assessment, because protecting a significant investment means understanding what is actually holding the structure up.

## What Determines Retrofit Scope

Every retrofit conversation starts with the same question: what is the home's existing structural system, and how does it perform under seismic load?

### Foundation type matters most

Homes on raised, cripple-wall foundations — common in older Los Angeles construction — are typically the most straightforward retrofit candidates, since foundation bolting and cripple wall bracing address well-understood failure modes. Homes on slab foundations or with more complex structural systems require a different, more individualized engineering evaluation. A raised foundation home from the 1920s through the 1960s, for instance, frequently has a mudsill that was never bolted to the foundation at all — the structure is essentially resting on the foundation under its own weight, which is exactly the condition that allows a house to slide off its foundation during strong ground shaking.

### Era of construction shapes the starting point

Homes built before modern seismic code requirements were adopted often lack basic connections between the structure and its foundation that are now standard. Understanding when and how a home was originally built is a key input into scoping retrofit work. Homes built prior to the mid-1970s predate many of the seismic provisions homeowners now take for granted, and even homes built through the 1990s may lack specific details — hold-downs at shear wall ends, properly nailed sheathing — that current code requires.

### Soil and site conditions factor in too

A structural engineer's seismic assessment doesn't stop at the foundation connection — soil type and site conditions affect how ground motion transmits to the structure. Homes on fill soil, or near a fault zone identified under the Alquist-Priolo Earthquake Fault Zoning Act, may warrant additional geotechnical consideration alongside the structural retrofit itself.

## What Retrofit Work Actually Involves

Retrofit scope ranges from targeted foundation work to more extensive structural intervention, depending on what the engineering assessment finds.

### Foundation bolting and cripple wall bracing

For homes with the common raised-foundation vulnerability, retrofit work typically includes bolting the structure to its foundation and bracing cripple walls — the short stud walls between the foundation and the first floor — to prevent lateral collapse. This work generally involves installing anchor bolts or foundation plates at regular intervals along the mudsill, and adding plywood shear paneling to the cripple wall to resist lateral movement, all of which can typically be done from crawl space access without disturbing finished living space above.

### Hillside and split-level homes need specialized engineering

Luxury homes built on hillside lots often sit on a combination of foundation types — a conventional foundation on the uphill side transitioning to raised posts, caissons, or a stepped foundation on the downhill side. These configurations don't fit standard retrofit prescriptions and require a custom engineering solution addressing how the different foundation types interact under seismic load.

### More complex homes may need broader structural intervention

Luxury homes with large open spans, extensive glazing, or architecturally complex structural systems may require more involved engineering solutions beyond standard foundation retrofit measures. A great room with floor-to-ceiling glass on two sides, for example, has far less wall area available for lateral bracing than a conventional floor plan, which often means steel moment frames or specially engineered shear elements are needed to achieve adequate seismic performance without sacrificing the architectural design.

## What a Retrofit Project Actually Costs and How Long It Takes

Cost varies enormously based on foundation type, home size, and access — a straightforward cripple wall bracing and bolting project on an accessible raised foundation is a modest undertaking measured in weeks, while a full structural upgrade involving new shear elements, steel framing, or foundation replacement on a larger or more architecturally complex home is a substantially larger undertaking that can take months and requires its own permitting and inspection sequence. Permitting for standard bolting-and-bracing retrofit work is often more streamlined than for other structural work, though projects involving more extensive intervention go through the same structural plan check process as any other significant structural modification.

## Why Combining Retrofit With a Remodel Often Makes Sense

Retrofit work and remodel work frequently overlap in the areas of the home they need to access.

### Shared access reduces disruption and cost

If a remodel already involves opening walls, floors, or crawl space areas, incorporating retrofit work into the same phase avoids opening those same areas twice — once for the remodel and again for a separate retrofit project. A primary suite remodel that already involves removing drywall down to the studs, for instance, is a natural opportunity to add hold-downs or shear paneling that would otherwise require cutting into finished walls specifically for that purpose.

### Engineering coordination benefits both scopes

A structural engineer evaluating a home for remodel-related changes is well positioned to also assess seismic performance at the same time, producing a single coordinated plan instead of two disconnected ones. This also tends to be more cost-efficient than commissioning two separate engineering evaluations months or years apart.

### Insurance and disclosure considerations

Some insurers offer more favorable terms for homes with documented seismic retrofit work, and a completed, permitted retrofit is also a meaningful disclosure item in a future sale — buyers and their lenders increasingly ask about foundation bolting status on older homes, particularly in higher-risk areas.

## What an Engineering Assessment Actually Involves

A proper seismic evaluation starts with a site visit where the engineer documents the existing foundation type, framing condition, and any visible signs of prior settlement or movement — cracked stucco, doors that no longer close squarely, gaps at baseboards. From there, the engineer typically develops calculations showing how the existing structure performs under current seismic design loads, and identifies specific deficiencies along with recommended remedies. For a straightforward raised-foundation home, this process moves relatively quickly; for a complex, architecturally ambitious luxury home, the assessment itself can take real time and may involve destructive investigation — opening a section of wall or floor — to verify assumptions about existing framing that aren't visible from a standard walkthrough.

## Permitting for Seismic Retrofit Work

Standard bolting and cripple wall bracing retrofit work can often move through a more streamlined permit track than a full structural remodel, since it follows well-established prescriptive standards that LADBS reviews efficiently. More extensive retrofit scope — new shear walls, steel moment frames, foundation replacement — is reviewed as full structural work, requiring the same stamped engineering calculations and inspection sequence as any other significant structural modification. Either way, retrofit work requires a permit; unpermitted structural work of this nature is exactly the kind of issue that surfaces during a future sale's inspection and can complicate both financing and disclosure.
