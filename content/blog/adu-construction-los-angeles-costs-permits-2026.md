An **ADU in Los Angeles** is one of the most requested projects we see — for rental income, for aging parents, for adult children, or simply for the flexibility of a second livable structure on the lot. The demand is real. So is the gap between what homeowners expect an ADU to cost and what a fully permitted, code-compliant unit actually requires.

Our [custom home construction](/services/custom-home-construction-los-angeles) team treats ADUs the same way we treat any ground-up structure: as a real building with its own foundation, systems, and code obligations — not a scaled-down room addition.

## What Drives ADU Cost in 2026

A detached ADU is, structurally, a small house. It needs its own foundation, roof, electrical panel or sub-panel, plumbing connections, and — depending on the jurisdiction and lot — fire separation and utility capacity upgrades.

### Detached vs. attached vs. conversion

Detached ADUs cost the most per square foot because they start from bare ground. Attached ADUs (built onto or above an existing structure) share some systems with the primary home, which can reduce cost. Garage or interior conversions are typically the least expensive path, since much of the shell and foundation already exists — but older garages often need seismic, electrical, and insulation upgrades to meet current code as a habitable space.

### Utilities are the most common budget surprise

Owners frequently underestimate the cost of bringing power, water, and sewer to a detached unit — especially on lots where the panel is already near capacity or the sewer lateral is old. We check utility capacity early in design specifically to avoid a mid-project surprise.

## How ADU Permitting Actually Works in LA

California's statewide ADU law requires local building departments to review complete ADU applications on an accelerated timeline compared to standard discretionary permits. That helps, but it does not remove LA-specific requirements around setbacks, height, parking, and fire access.

### Standard plans move faster

LADBS offers a standard plan program for pre-approved ADU designs, which can shorten plan check meaningfully compared to a fully custom design. For owners prioritizing speed over a bespoke layout, this is worth evaluating early.

### Non-standard lots slow things down

Hillside lots, lots with easements, narrow lots, or properties near a fire hazard severity zone often require additional review. The earlier these constraints are identified, the less redesign is needed later.

## What a Realistic ADU Timeline Looks Like

Most ADU projects run through three phases: design and plan check, permitting, and construction. Plan check is often the least predictable phase, since it depends on department workload and how complete the original submission is.

### Design decisions made early save time later

Finalizing unit size, utility connections, and exterior materials before submitting for permit reduces the number of correction cycles. Owners who change the design mid-review restart part of the clock.

### Construction sequencing matters

Once permitted, a detached ADU generally moves through foundation, framing, rough systems, inspections, and finishes in a sequence similar to a small home — with inspections gating each phase.
